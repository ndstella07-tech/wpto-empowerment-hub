# WPTO Empowerment Hub

Build a modern, persuasive, factual website for WOMEN PALM TREE ORGANISATION (WPTO).

Use the website patterns of UNHCR, CARE, and Heifer as references for structure only: a strong mission-led hero, clear calls to action, impact sections, story sections, program sections, trust signals, and simple navigation. Do not copy their wording, colors, or branding. Use a calm, clean, humanitarian look with a soft, low-contrast color palette. Do not use harsh color contrast. Do not use flashy gradients, loud neon tones, or visual clutter.

The website must feel trustworthy, human, and professional. It must communicate hope, dignity, action, and community support.

Core facts to use:
WPTO is a registered Malawian organisation based in Dowa, at Dzaleka Refugee Camp, formed in 2020. It works with women, single mothers, widows, youth, children, refugees, and vulnerable communities. Its focus is rural livelihoods, agriculture, food and nutrition security, climate resilience, skills development, and access to finance and markets. Its work is aligned with SDG 2 and SDG 17 and with Malawi development priorities.

Website goal:
The site must make people understand what WPTO does, why it matters, and how they can support it.

Tone:
Clear, factual, respectful, and persuasive.
Write in simple English.
Avoid vague language.
Avoid exaggerated claims.
Every claim should be supportable and specific.
The content should feel real, grounded, and credible.

Visual direction:
Use large hero sections with strong imagery.
Use real people, farming, women’s empowerment, food, community, and resilience themes.
Use short text blocks.
Use clear spacing.
Use cards for programs, outcomes, impact, and partners.
Use icons only when useful.
Use one primary CTA style across the site.
Use a consistent button style.
Use mobile-first design.
Use readable typography.
Use generous whitespace.
Use subtle animations only.
Do not overload the homepage with too many sections.
Do not use busy layouts.

Strict building rules:

Put the most important message above the fold.

The hero section must say who WPTO is, what it does, and why it matters in one glance.

Put one clear primary CTA in the hero and one secondary CTA.

Use factual copy only. No invented statistics.

If a number is not given, do not invent it.

Use WPTO’s real location and focus areas.

Show proof of work through impact, programs, partners, and stories.

Make the donation, volunteer, partnership, and contact paths easy to find.

Keep the navigation simple and predictable.

Every page must end with a clear action.

Avoid overdesign. The layout should feel structured, calm, and trustworthy.

Use low-contrast color pairing. Do not force strong contrast between background and text blocks unless needed for accessibility.

Build for clarity first, beauty second.

Do not use long paragraphs when a short one works better.

Use headings that are direct and meaningful.

Recommended site skeleton:

Header:
Logo on the left.
Navigation links: Home, About, Our Work, Impact, Stories, Partners, Contact.
A top-right CTA button: Support WPTO.

Homepage:
Hero section:
Headline about empowering women, widows, single mothers, refugees, and vulnerable communities.
Subheadline about agriculture, food security, nutrition, resilience, and livelihoods in Malawi.
Primary CTA: Support the Mission.
Secondary CTA: Explore Our Work.

Section 1: About WPTO
Brief introduction to who WPTO is, where it works, and why it exists.

Section 2: What We Do
Use 3 to 5 cards only.
Cards should cover:
Food and nutrition support
Agriculture and irrigation
Livelihoods and skills
Refugee and host community support
Women’s empowerment and resilience

Section 3: Our Impact / Outcomes
Show WPTO’s three main program outcomes in simple form:
Food and nutrition security
Improved nutrition, health, and education
Climate-resilient and sustainable livelihoods

Section 4: How We Work
Explain the approach in plain language:
partnerships, local solutions, community support, training, and resilience building.

Section 5: Stories or Field Updates
Use one or two short story cards showing human impact.

Section 6: Partners
Show government, NGO, development, and community partnership direction.

Section 7: Call to Action
Strong closing message with buttons:
Donate
Volunteer
Partner With Us
Contact Us

About page:
Who we are
Vision
Mission
Where we work
Why WPTO was formed
Values

Our Work page:
Break down the three outcomes into clear program areas.
For each program area, show:
Goal
Who it serves
Main activities
Expected result

Impact page:
Use factual language.
Show achievements only if provided.
If no verified numbers exist, present qualitative impact statements.

Stories page:
Use real, human-centered story templates.
Focus on women, households, farmers, refugees, and communities.
Make stories short, practical, and hopeful.

Partners page:
Explain how partners can support WPTO.
Show partnership types:
Funding
Technical support
Program delivery
Agricultural support
Community support

Contact page:
Show location in Dowa.
Show contact form.
Show email, phone, and map placeholder if available.
Add a clear invitation to get in touch.

Design rules for content:
Keep language simple.
Use active voice.
Use short sentences.
Prefer “we support”, “we work with”, “we help”, “we build”.
Avoid charity language that sounds weak or pity-based.
Use dignity-first language.
Make women look like leaders, farmers, builders, and decision-makers.
Make the organization feel community-led and solution-driven.

Copy direction for the hero:
WPTO empowers women, widows, single mothers, refugees, and vulnerable communities in Malawi through agriculture, nutrition, and resilient livelihoods.
Buttons:
Support the Mission
See Our Work

Copy direction for the closing CTA:
Join us in building stronger households, safer food systems, and more resilient communities in Dowa and beyond.

Deliverables:
Create the full website structure.
Write the homepage copy.
Write clear section headings.
Write persuasive but factual content.
Keep the design elegant, minimal, and strong.
Avoid color contrast clashes.
Make the site feel credible enough for donors, partners, and the public.
